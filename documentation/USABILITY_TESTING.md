## 1. Usability Testing Plan (From Project Specification)

- **Goal of the test:** Find out whether real elderly users can complete MediRemind's 4 main tasks independently without help, and identify where they hesitate or get confused.
- **Participants:** 3 to 5 elderly users (aged 60+, ideally different people from your initial 5 interview participants).
- **Location:** A quiet, well-lit room. Open the MediRemind prototype on a smartphone (or laptop browser resized to mobile view).
- **Before Each User:** Open **Help (`?`) → Reset Sample Demo Data** inside MediRemind so every participant starts with the exact same clean state (where *Panadol at 8 pm* is not yet added, *Norvasc* is due, and *Yesterday* has sample taken/missed records).

### How to Run One Test (~15 Minutes)
1. **Welcome & Reassure:** Say *"Assalam-o-Alaikum. Remember, we are testing the app, not you. You cannot make a mistake—if something is hard, that means our design needs to be improved."*
2. **Permission:** Ask permission to take notes and measure task time.
3. **Think-Aloud Instructions:** Ask them to speak their thoughts aloud while using the app:
   - *English:* *"Please say out loud what you are looking at and what you expect to happen when you press a button."*
   - *Roman Urdu:* *"Jo bhi aap ke zehen mein aa raha ho bol kar batayein—maslan aap kaunsa button dhoondh rahe hain ya kis cheez se confusion ho rahi hai."*
4. **Give One Task at a Time:** Read each task clearly. **Do not help or point to the screen** unless the user is completely stuck and asks to stop.
5. **Observe & Record:** Write down whether they completed the task (`Yes/No`), time in seconds, number of errors/wrong taps, and comments.
6. **SUS Questionnaire:** At the end, read the 10 SUS statements aloud if helpful and record ratings from 1 to 5.

## 5. Exact SUS Score Calculation Method

Follow these 4 steps for **each user**:

1. **For odd-numbered statements (1, 3, 5, 7, 9):** subtract 1 from the user's score → `(score - 1)`
2. **For even-numbered statements (2, 4, 6, 8, 10):** subtract the user's score from 5 → `(5 - score)`
3. **Add all 10 converted numbers** together (possible total: `0` to `40`).
4. **Multiply the total by `2.5`** to convert it to a `0` to `100` scale.

$$\text{SUS} = 2.5 \times \left[ \sum_{\text{odd}} (x_i - 1) + \sum_{\text{even}} (5 - x_i) \right]$$

**Formula Example (from Project PDF — for learning only, NOT real data):**
- Suppose a user's 10 converted scores add up to `32`.
- Multiply: `32 × 2.5 = 80`.
- **Interpretation:** Above **68** is above average usability. Above **80** is very good usability.
